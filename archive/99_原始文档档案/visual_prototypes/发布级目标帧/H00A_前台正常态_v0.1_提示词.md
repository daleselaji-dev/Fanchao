4. 不出现任何怪物、异世界、反常雾墙或明显灵异事件。

## 生成提示词

Use case: photorealistic-natural

Asset type: 16:9 high-fidelity live-action horror-game base plate, H00-A pre-crisis normal state, final production target rather than moodboard.

Input images:

- Image 1 is the rendering-quality reference only: preserve its weighty foreground objects, layered low-key practical lighting, believable damp material response, CRT glass and deep spatial readability. Do not copy its atrium composition and do not include the creature shown on its television.
- Image 2 is the millennium-era authenticity reference only: preserve its lived-in early-2000s Chinese wedding-hotel density, mirrored brass trim, red chair covers, warm-white ceiling fixtures, service ware, plastic flowers, ordinary staff clothing and documentary photographic truth. Do not copy its exact banquet-room composition and do not create an infinite-mirror effect.

Primary request: Show the front desk of a locally owned Chinese seaside wedding hotel around 2000–2003, approximately ten minutes before the crisis. This is the first explorable game space. It must feel specific, operational and slightly oppressive even though nothing supernatural is visible yet.

Camera/composition: Camera stands just inside the staff side opening of the reception counter at normal gameplay eye height, 35 mm lens. The L-shaped wooden counter occupies the lower foreground and one side, creating tactile depth without blocking movement. The large wooden room-key and message cubby rack is the visual center, close enough that individual brass room numbers, leather key tags and folded message slips are readable as objects. A small convex CRT security monitor and a red/black multiline telephone console sit on the counter but do not dominate the rack. A staff-only passage and part of the public lobby are visible beyond, giving at least two plausible exploration routes.

Hotel layout: A modest but ambitious local wedding hotel, not a luxury international chain. Dark reddish varnished plywood counter with worn brass edging; cream walls with nicotine and humidity staining; brown-red patterned carpet; locally made granite or faux-marble skirting; mirrored wall panels with slightly tarnished edges; low ceiling over the desk opening, taller public lobby beyond. The architecture should feel expanded and renovated in stages, with mismatched trim and practical cable routing.

Millennium-era operational details: Physical brass room keys with heavy dark-red or brown leather fobs; wooden pigeonholes for keys and paper messages; handwritten shift ledger; carbon-copy guest registration forms; dot-matrix continuous paper with perforated edges; wired red desk telephone and a separate black multiline console; small curved-screen CCTV monitor with four low-resolution black-and-white camera quadrants; calculator with large keys; ashtray with no active cigarette; thermos flask and enamel mug; ballpoint pens on chains; wall calendar from an indistinct local business; simple plastic desk clock; stack of banquet menus; mechanical service bell. No modern flat screens, smartphones, card terminals, minimalist décor or fashionable retro props.

People: One ordinary front-desk employee in practical early-2000s local hotel work clothes is finishing a phone call and checking a handwritten room card. Two wedding guests or waiting relatives are visible in the public side, behaving casually and not facing camera. Through a side opening or one ordinary mirror, only a partial glimpse of the active wedding banquet appears: red chair covers, servers, dishes and warm crowd movement. The banquet is supporting life context, not the main subject.

CRT content: Mundane four-way hotel CCTV only—entrance in sea fog, a guest corridor, a service stair and part of the lobby. No monster, no future event, no static face and no readable text. The monitor is present because it will become important in later states.

Lighting/mood: Practical mixed lighting only. Warm-white fluorescent and inexpensive ceiling fixtures over the occupied counter, weak cold gray daylight filtered through real coastal fog at the entrance, small tungsten desk lamp accents. Slight underexposure in corners, readable blacks, natural highlight rolloff on wet varnish and CRT glass. A quiet sense that the desk is holding too many rooms and too many small obligations, but no cinematic horror spotlight, no blue-orange blockbuster grade.

Surface truth: Finger-polished telephone plastic, water rings on varnished wood, paper softened by humidity, fingerprints on CRT glass, oxidized brass, carpet compressed along staff paths, swollen veneer seams, condensation at the entrance-side window, dust trapped at mirror edges. Photographic skin, ordinary fabric and imperfect local construction.

Color palette: Deep brown-red wood, dull brass, nicotine cream, burgundy carpet, muted warm white, gray coastal daylight, small faded red accents. Restrained saturation; no monochrome gray horror filter.

Critical constraints:

- no monster, corpse, body fragment, blood, gore, organic wall, swollen flesh, shell, coral, tentacle or prehistoric organism;
- no impossible room depth yet, no portal cubbies, no floating keys and no supernatural distortion;
- no giant open atrium dominating the shot, no fog plane cutting the room, no rust world, no hanging biological altar;
- no modern luxury hotel, no Western grand-hotel aesthetic, no generic 1980s America, no Japanese ryokan, no Soviet institutional corridor;
- no polished concept-art look, game-poster composition, fisheye lens, extreme Dutch angle or theatrical spotlight;
- no infinite mirrors; at most one ordinary hotel mirror carrying a partial banquet reflection;
- no readable brand logos, title, UI overlay, subtitle, watermark or extra text;
- maintain clear navigable floor and human-scale counter openings suitable for a playable scene.

Success criterion: The viewer should immediately recognize a real Chinese wedding hotel around the millennium and want to inspect its keys, telephone, CRT and paperwork. The frame must be visually rich before any horror is added, because all later anomalies will be judged against this exact ordinary reality.
