# M01「多房间连续体」视觉原型 v0.1 提示词

## 原型要回答的问题

1. 玩家能否一眼看出：这不是多个人，也不是尸体拼贴，而是**同一具普通人体被酒店房间分配到多个位置**？
2. 直接视野与镜中全貌的矛盾，是否足以制造恐怖，而不依靠血浆、长肢、怪脸和海洋装饰？
3. 画面能否直接转译成游戏：固定门锚点、人体分段、遮挡蒙版、镜面／CRT 预渲染？

## 生成提示词

Use case: photorealistic-natural

Asset type: 16:9 high-fidelity horror-game target frame, live-action photographic look, gameplay camera rather than poster art.

Input images: Image 1 and Image 2 are visual continuity references only for the same early-2000s Chinese coastal hotel, damp materials, practical lighting, restrained gray sea fog, realistic production design and cinematic photographic finish. Do not copy their lobby composition and do not copy the creature visible inside the television.

Primary request: Create the first unmistakable encounter with an active humanoid threat in a guest-room corridor of the same hotel. The horror comes from one ordinary human body remaining physically continuous while different portions of it occupy several separate hotel rooms. Direct vision shows only locally plausible body portions at different doorways; a large wall mirror reveals that all portions belong to one complete, painfully folded body passing through impossible room depth.

Scene/backdrop: Second-floor guest corridor in a Chinese seaside wedding hotel around 2000–2003. Worn patterned carpet, cream wallpaper darkened by damp, varnished wooden room doors with small brass number plates, weak warm wall lamps, dirty white ceiling, subtle low sea fog entering from the stairwell. No people except the single threat. The corridor is still recognizably real and usable, not an abstract dream set.

Camera/composition: Third-person gameplay-height camera, approximately 35 mm lens, looking diagonally down a long corridor. Four doorways and a large aged wall mirror are readable in the same frame. The player character is absent. Keep enough clear floor and doorway geometry to understand movement routes.

Direct-view body evidence:

- Door A, nearest: the back of an ordinary wet dark-green jacket and one side of a human head lean partly out, initially readable as a normal hotel guest seen from behind.
- Door B, farther down: a forearm wearing the exact same jacket sleeve emerges naturally from the narrow opening and grips the doorframe; there is no severed stump and no visible connecting tube.
- Door C: one hand from that same body depresses the brass handle from inside.
- At the corridor bend: two ordinary trousered legs bear weight and begin one step, but the torso is not locally present.
- Architecture occludes every impossible connection. No body segment floats in open space. No gore at the occlusion boundaries.

Mirror revelation: The large mirror shows the entire scene from a contradictory angle. In the mirror only, all visible portions connect into ONE complete adult human body with exactly one head, two arms and two legs. The body is not elastically stretched; instead it is repeatedly folded through the impossible depth of multiple rooms, like one exhausted person attempting to stand in several rooms at once. Shoulder rotation, jacket seams and fabric folds continue consistently across the hidden distances. The complete mirrored posture should be anatomically painful, visually readable and immediately threatening, but still recognizably human.

Human appearance: Ordinary adult hotel guest, average build, early-2000s practical clothes, wet heavy jacket, dark trousers and worn leather shoes. Skin has irregular drowning-like edema, pale pressure marks and subtle delayed rebound; pores, wet hair and fabric weight are photographic. The face remains a believable human face suffering severe inner-ear pressure, not a monster mask. Do not glamourize.

Action cue: Several door handles are caught at slightly different stages of turning, implying one movement propagating down the corridor. The nearest visible shoulder is beginning to withdraw while the farther hand is already preparing the next doorway. The viewer should understand that opening more doors gives this body more places to move.

Lighting/mood: Practical tungsten wall lamps with uneven exposure, weak cold ambient sea-fog light from the stairwell, wet carpet reflections kept subtle. The corridor beyond the mirror is darker. Quiet, watchful dread before sudden movement; no action-movie lighting and no theatrical spotlight on the creature.

Color/materials: Nicotine cream, old burgundy carpet, oxidized brass, water-darkened green cloth, gray-green skin undertones, dirty mirror silvering, damp wallpaper seams. Real hotel wear, fingerprints, mineral bloom and small moisture beads. Avoid glossy creature surfaces.

Critical readability constraints:

- exactly one person and exactly one human anatomy: one head, two arms, two legs;
- direct view must show separated local portions at multiple doors, while the mirror must clearly connect them into one body;
- no open-space elastic stretching between doors;
- no cuts, collage edges, photo-montage look or duplicate clones;
- no extra limbs, extra faces, conjoined bodies or pile of corpses;
- no tentacles, shells, coral, fish anatomy, insect anatomy, biomechanical pipes or alien exoskeleton;
- no long-haired crawling ghost, no giant grin, black eyes, split jaw, exposed brain or generic zombie infection;
- no television head, door head, object replacement face or symbolic costume;
- no excessive blood, entrails, exposed bones or flesh-wall transformation;
- no text overlays, title, interface, subtitles, watermark or concept-sheet labels.

Success criterion: Without reading lore, the image should make the viewer first think “there is a person at several doors,” then realize through the mirror “it is one body using the hotel itself as the missing distance.” It must feel feasible as a high-end in-engine horror encounter using one rig, several door anchors and a pre-rendered mirror view.
